Slip of current-driven skyrmion lattices beyond the Doppler threshold
 · 
Abstract
A spin-polarized current drives a chiral magnetic texture through a Doppler (Landau) instability once the effective drift u exceeds u_c = ω₀(k_min)/(λ k_min). For the gyrotropic phonons of a skyrmion lattice (SkX), ω₀ ∝ k², so u_c ∝ 1/L_x and an extended lattice is unstable at any current. We ask what happens beyond this threshold, using Landau–Lifshitz–Gilbert simulations of a Néel SkX and a chiral helix with Zhang–Li torques.
The two textures end differently. A helix runs away from its threshold into phase slips, giving a winding-number staircase with topological hysteresis. A SkX first saturates on a continuous branch, a steadily moving strain wave whose amplitude depends only on u/u_c (scale invariance, verified for L_x = 76–608). It then slips when the wave's shear plus compression exceed the local Born stability of the homogeneously deformed lattice.
The finite-size correction to this local criterion is the zero-point cost of confining the soft mode in a scale-invariant harmonic margin well, which gives Δγ_c ∝ 1/L. Three consequences follow. The slip threshold is independent of damping. A uniaxial strain ε₀ shifts it quantitatively (d ln u_s/dε₀ ≈ −2.2). The tilt of the slipped lattice is fixed by the skyrmion charge and is independent of the current direction, as required by the C₂z and M_y symmetries.
I. Introduction
Current-driven chiral textures have a pattern analogue of Walker breakdown: a static or rigidly drifting pattern turns unstable when the Doppler shift of its soft modes exceeds their frequency. A companion paper (B1) derives this threshold and its efficiency λ from the symmetry of nonreciprocal spin couplings. The present paper asks the next question: what does the pattern do beyond the threshold, and what sets where it finally breaks?
Two textures answer differently.
Helix. Its Goldstone mode is a phase, and its threshold u_L = D/√2 is finite. The instability grows without saturation and ends in phase slips, which reduce the winding number one step at a time.
Skyrmion lattice. Its threshold vanishes as 1/L. The lattice first carries a saturated strain wave and slips only later, when a lattice-scale elastic mode fails.
We show that both endings obey one rule: slip occurs where the locally, homogeneously deformed pattern loses static stability. For the helix this is local unwinding; for the SkX it is a Born instability of the sheared and compressed lattice.
The main results are:
Universal strain curve. Beyond u_c, the SkX strain amplitude is a universal function of u/u_c, a consequence of a scale invariance of the gyrotropic phase dynamics.
Local Born criterion. The wave carries a fixed compression ε_xx = −0.19γ set by its elliptical polarization. The static Born threshold of that state, γ_s(H_peak) = 0.269, is the L → ∞ slip threshold.
Harmonic finite-size correction. The correction is Δγ_c ≈ ℓ_K√m₂ ∝ 1/L, the zero-point cost of confining the soft mode in a scale-invariant margin well. It is independent of damping.
Strain tuning. A uniaxial strain moves the threshold by dγ_c/dε₀ = 0.44, equal to the static dγ_s/dε_xx. This is a direct test of locality.
Tilt selection rule. The tilt of the slipped lattice is selected by the field sign and not by the current sign, as the C₂z and M_y symmetries require.
Weak barriers. Hysteresis at T = 0 is topological (winding, Bragg indices, frozen defects), but its barriers are a few percent of J.
II. Model and the linear threshold
We use a square-lattice classical spin model with exchange J = 1, interfacial (Néel) DMI D = 0.4 and field B = 0.5D²/J, which stabilizes a triangular SkX. In-plane Zhang–Li torques have adiabatic and non-adiabatic parts. Unless stated otherwise, α = 0.3 and β = 0.6, so that u_eff = (β/α − 1)u = u. The lattice displacement u(r, t) obeys gyrotropic (type-B) Goldstone dynamics, G ẑ×∂_t u + 𝒟∂_t u = −δE′/δu. The pseudo-energy E′ = F_el[∇u] − λu_eff Π_x contains the crystal momentum Π_x = (G/2)∫(ẑ×u)·∂_x u. The phonon frequency is ω₀(k) = √(C_L C_T) k²/G. The Landau condition ω₀(k) < λu_eff k_x is first met at the smallest allowed k_x = 2π/L_x:
The simulations give u_c·L_x = 7.56–7.58 for L_x = 76–608 (0.3% scatter) and no dependence on L_y. With the measured ω₀/k² = 0.616, this implies λ = 0.511, within 1% of the independently computed dissipative Doppler overlap κ_P = 0.505 (paper B1). The elastic constants C_T = 0.0137 and C_L = 0.0449 give √(C_L C_T)/G = 0.647, 5% from the measured ω₀/k². An infinite lattice is therefore unstable at any current; the physics of interest is what happens beyond u_c.
III. Beyond threshold: phase slips versus a saturated branch
Helix: a winding staircase. We take a chain of N = 200 sites with D = J tan(2π·3/200), so that the ground state holds n = 3 turns.
No saturation. Just above the linear threshold u_L(3) = 0.0707 the mode grows exponentially without saturating, accelerates, and ends in a phase slip, n = 3 → 2.
Staircase. Raising u gives the steps 3 → 2 → 1 → 0. They sit at the linear thresholds of each winding, u_L(2) = 0.1094 and u_unw(1) = 0.158.
Topological hysteresis. On lowering u, re-winding from the ferromagnet is reversible near u_unw(1), but n = 1 survives down to u = 0.
The slip criterion is local unwinding. A helix of local pitch q unwinds when u exceeds
which decreases with q. The phase mode has no restoring nonlinearity, so local compression grows until the most compressed region crosses q*(u), defined by u_unw(q*) = u. Past that point growth accelerates by a factor 1.4–5 and a slip follows. At u_L ≈ D/√2 this requires 25–30% compression. Longer chains (N = 600, 1200) follow u_L(n) on average but lose 2–4 turns at once. Strips (L_y = 8–32) slip uniformly across their width, because the fastest mode has k_y = 0.
SkX: a continuous branch. A 4×1 supercell (76×33 sites, 8 skyrmions) bifurcates supercritically at u_c = 0.0994.
Single attractor. For u/u_c = 1.03, 1.06 and 1.10, runs started from the perfect lattice and from a strongly deformed state converge to the same steady amplitude.
Steady moving state. The final state is a moving lattice that carries a steady strain wave and keeps every skyrmion.
No mobility drop. The drift velocity stays on the Thiele value to 10⁻⁴, unlike Walker breakdown.
Registry as a winding number. Write the triple-q lattice as density waves ψ_a = |ψ|e^{iφ_a}, with φ_a = G_a·(r − u) and a = 1, 2, 3. In a periodic box the Bragg indices
are integers, the exact two-dimensional analogue of the helix winding (1/2π)∮∂_xφ dx. They change only when a dislocation, a zero of ψ_a, crosses the cycle C_j; the change is ΔN_a = ±G_a·b/2π. A registry slip is therefore the SkX counterpart of a helix phase slip.
We detect it topologically, through Burgers circuits on the Delaunay graph and box-cycle mismatches of the unwrapped phases. Reading the index from the strongest Fourier peak fails once G·U ≳ 1.5, because sidebands then outweigh the Bragg peak. That reading misclassifies intact large-amplitude states as slipped.

V. Slip is a local Born instability
Static map. We impose a uniform H = γŷx̂ᵀ + ε_xx x̂x̂ᵀ through a twisted periodic cell, S(x + L_x, y) = S(x, y − s), and diagonalize the Bloch Jacobian of the relaxed state.
Long-wavelength first. The instability appears first at k → 0, with λ ≈ c(γ, θ)k² along θ ≈ 116°–124°. It is the loss of positivity of an incremental elastic modulus: a Born instability, not the softening of a finite-k phonon.
No collapse before it. Skyrmion number is conserved up to γ = 0.53, so no core-collapse or strip-out boundary precedes it.
Not a stress maximum. The shear energy stiffens with γ, so this is not a Frenkel stress maximum either.

The well itself is scale invariant: its position is ∝ L and its curvature m₂ ∝ 1/L². The excess margin at threshold approaches m_max ≈ 27/L.
Candidates. Three mechanisms could produce a size-dependent correction.
Advection. The wave moves through the lattice at v_rel ∝ 1/L. For a local instability in a flowing medium, the absolute-instability condition shifts the threshold by ∝ v_rel²/(Γ′K). That shift is ∝ 1/L² and depends on damping through Γ′, so it fails on both counts.
Current Doppler term. The current acting on the local mode also enters relative to a kinetic rate, so it too would depend on damping. The uniform-cell threshold changes by only 1% up to u = 0.1, so it fails quantitatively.
Static non-uniformity. Only this candidate is compatible with the damping independence of Sec. V.

The unstable wave travels along the current and is mainly transverse, so slip changes the Bragg indices N_a^{(x)} of the cycle along the current. In 8×1 and 8×2 boxes the change is m = (+1, −2) in the a₁, a₂ basis, a tilt of the lattice. All 22 slipped runs obey one rule:

The two 8×2 runs not listed did not slip within 6×10⁵ time units. The rule follows from exact symmetries of the model, with spins transforming as axial vectors.
C₂z. (x, y) → (−x, −y) and (S_x, S_y, S_z) → (−S_x, −S_y, S_z). Exchange, Néel DMI and B_z are invariant; only u → −u. A tilted lattice is C₂z-invariant because its Bragg set is closed under G → −G. Reversing the current therefore gives the same tilt.
M_y. y → −y. Here u_x is invariant, Néel DMI is invariant, and the axial field flips, B_z → −B_z. Reversing the field therefore gives the mirror-image tilt.
No third symmetry. No symmetry of the model flips the tilt at fixed (u, B). The selection is deterministic, not spontaneous.

The sense of the current-induced lattice rotation is set by the skyrmion charge and is even in the current. This is a direct experimental signature that separates the mechanism from gradient-driven rotations, which reverse with the current.
Velocity memory. There is no Walker-type mobility drop: while the original registry survives, V_x/u = 1.1588 and V_y/V_x = 0.319 hold to 10⁻⁴ across threshold. Velocity changes only stepwise, when the structure changes.
Tilted lattice: V_x −3.6%.
90°-rotated lattice: Hall ratio −17%.
In an undistorted triangular lattice, C₆ makes the dissipation tensor 𝒟 isotropic, so rotation alone cannot change the Hall angle. The observed change comes from the residual strain (C₆ → C₂) that the box imposes after the slip. In samples, boundaries and pinning play the role of the box, which predicts a Hall-angle memory proportional to residual strain.
VIII. Finite temperature and current memory
At T = 0 every slip leaves a memory that survives to u = 0: lost helix turns, a changed Bragg registry, or frozen dislocations. Thermal fluctuations shrink this memory but do not erase it on simulation time scales; the barriers are a few percent of J.
Helix (N = 200). The hysteresis area ∫(n_up − n_down)du falls from 0.21 (T = 0) to 0.15, 0.08, 0.055 and 0.03 at T = 0.002, 0.005, 0.01 and 0.02. Arrhenius fits of waiting times (6 seeds) give two barriers, each uncertain by about ±30%:
Re-winding barrier (n = 1 → 2): ΔE ≈ 0.033 J, nearly the same at u = 0 and u = 0.06. The current hardly lowers it, so recovery on the descending branch is set by temperature.
Slip barrier (n = 3 → 2) at 0.78 u_L: ΔE ≈ 0.015 J, vanishing as u → u_L.
SkX (8×2). Defects frozen at u = 0 persist for 3×10⁵ time units at T = 0.05. At T ≳ 0.07 ≈ 0.35 T_m they heal within 10⁴–10⁵, and ψ₆ recovers from 0.6 to 0.85–0.9. At T = 0.2 the lattice melts, consistent with the KTHNY melting temperature T_m ≈ 0.19–0.21 found in paper B1.
What this means for samples. In the monolayer model the barriers are a few percent of J. A slip or re-winding event in a film of N_z layers creates a defect line or wall through the thickness, so ΔE ∝ N_z is the natural expectation. If so, the memory survives near room temperature in films tens of layers thick. This scaling is not yet computed.
IX. Experimental implications
Where the model meets real samples. In a sample, L_x is not the system size but the length over which the lattice responds coherently along the current: a pinning or grain length, or the sample size in a nanostructure. The predictions below involve only ratios to u_c or static elastic properties, so they do not depend on that length.
Bragg satellites, not drift velocity. Above u_c the lattice carries a strain wave, and its satellite intensity rises as ∝ (u − u_c). The mean drift and Hall angle stay on their Thiele values until a slip.
Strain tunes the slip current. A uniaxial strain along the current shifts u_c through the elastic constants (d ln u_c/dε₀ ≈ −1.5) and u_s/u_c through the Born criterion.

X. Conclusion
Beyond the Doppler threshold, a driven chiral texture fails where its locally deformed state loses static stability.
Helix. The threshold is finite, so the phase mode runs directly into local unwinding.
Skyrmion lattice. The threshold vanishes as 1/L, and scale invariance first produces a universal strain wave. The lattice slips when that wave's shear plus compression cross the Born boundary.
Finite size. The correction is a static harmonic zero-point cost.
Selection. The tilt is selected by the C₂z and M_y symmetries.
Current therefore decides how strongly the lattice is deformed, and the static lattice decides where it breaks.
Appendix: methods, figures, open checks
Numerics. All simulations are classical spin dynamics on a square lattice with periodic boundaries.
Integration. Heun integration with dt = 0.2 (dt = 0.1 for α = 0.15). The time step is verified by halving it; growth rates agree to 10⁻³.
Boxes. n_x × n_y copies of the 19×33 two-skyrmion cell, from 4×1 to 32×8 (L_x = 76–608).
Protocol. Quasi-static current ramps with a 10⁻⁶ noise re-seed at each step. Each step is held to equilibrium (< 1% change over the last half).
Registry. Topological, from Bragg phases: Burgers circuits plus box-cycle index changes.
Static stability. Twisted periodic cells, sparse shift-invert Bloch diagonalization, and the long-wavelength coefficient c(γ, θ).
Floquet. Nonlinear finite-difference propagation of 6 vectors over T = a/v_rel with QR re-orthogonalization, with neutral directions projected out.
